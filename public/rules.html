The complete rules · reads in four minutes

How to spill.

Three or four players, seven cards each, one deck of a hundred. Be the first to empty your hand. Everything else is detail, and the detail is below.

Four suits: Ember, Tide, Moss and Sol. Each has its own colour and mark, so the cards stay readable for colour-blind players.

2

The deal and the turn

Everyone gets seven cards. The top card of the pile is turned up; if it's an action, it goes back under the deck until a number shows. A random player starts.

On your turn, play a card that matches the top card by suit or by number. Action cards also match the same action in any suit. A prism plays on anything.

If you can't play, or would rather not, draw one card. If it fits, you may play it straight away. Otherwise your turn ends. A turn clock keeps things moving; if it runs out, you draw automatically.

3

The spill

After you play a number, you may keep going with more numbers of the same suit, each one step higher or each one step lower than the last. You can't change direction mid-run, and there is no wrapping from 9 to 1.

A spill of three or more cards splashes the next player, who draws one card before their turn.

4

Action cards

  • Surge. The next player draws two and loses their turn. They can dodge it by playing a surge of their own, which adds two more and passes the bill along. (Hosts can switch stacking off.)
  • Eddy. Reverses the direction of play.
  • Gift. Choose one of your other cards and hand it to the next player. (Hosts can take gifts out of the deck.)
  • Prism. Wild. Play it on anything and name the suit that follows. While a surge is pending, a prism does not dodge it.
5

Winning and rating

The first player with no cards wins the match and scores the value of every card still held by the others: numbers count their face value, actions 10, prisms 20.

Matches are rated with an Elo system. Beating stronger players moves your rating more. House bots play at a fixed 1300.
