A card game for three or four  /  live tables  /  bots that learn

Play it in runs. Empty your hand first.

Spillway is a shedding game with one twist. When your cards climb or fall in a single suit, you lay the whole run down in one turn. Spill three or more and the next player catches the overflow.

    Match the table.

    Lay a card that shares the suit or the number on top of the pile. Ember 4 is showing, so any Ember works, and so does any 4.

  2. 02

    Keep climbing.

    Holding Tide 5, 6 and 7 as well? Put them all down after the 4. One suit, one step at a time, up or down. That's a spill.

  3. 03

    Splash.

    A spill of three or more makes the next player draw a card. Short runs empty your hand. Long runs ruin somebody's evening.

Four cards that bend a round

Not every card is a number.

Surge · 8 in the deck

The next player draws two and loses their turn.

Unless they answer with a surge of their own. Then the bill doubles and moves on, and keeps moving until someone can't pay it.

Eddy · 8 in the deck

The table turns around.

Play passes the other way. The player who just went is suddenly next, and the one who was about to win has to wait.

Gift · 8 in the deck

Hand your worst card to the next player.

You choose which one. They have to take it. Best saved for the moment someone gets down to two cards.

Prism · 4 in the deck

The wild. Plays on anything.

Name the suit that follows. Holding one back for the last turn is usually smarter than spending it early. The bots worked that out on their own.

The house bots

The table learns.

Every finished match trains the bots that fill empty seats. A small linear model scores each legal move on seven traits, then shifts its weights toward whatever the winner did. Nothing is hidden: these are the live numbers from the server.
